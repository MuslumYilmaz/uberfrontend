Research behind it: `docs/references/interview-prep-market-research-2026-10.md` section 1 (what candidates pay for) and the competitor pricing pages read on 2026-10-15 (GreatFrontEnd, NeetCode, Hello Interview, Boot.dev; figures below). ## Why

No comparable entrant grew because of its price level. NeetCode (free explanations first, Pro years later), Hello Interview (free written guides first, Premium later) and Boot.dev (priced above Codecademy, 30-day no-questions refund) grew on distribution and credibility; the cheapest frontend bank, BigFrontEnd at $6 a month, did not grow. Against GreatFrontEnd our visible gaps are a $12 floor that lets a candidate consume the library in one month, a lifetime price above GreatFrontEnd's for a smaller library, no purchasing-power pricing (a candidate in Turkey pays GreatFrontEnd ₺2,300 a year and us $79), a refund policy that is decided case by case, and a premium promise that says "where available".

## Decisions recorded on 2026-10-15

Agreed:

1. Premium is sold as credibility, not as access. The pricing hero, proof chips, plan features and the Free Explorer table name what the gate contains today (sourced company practice pages, front-end system design walkthroughs with official-source checks, runnable workspaces with tests, guided solutions) and drop "where available". Copy names only what exists and passes `public-trust-regressions.test.mjs`.
2. Risk reversal. A first purchase on any plan is refundable in full within 14 days on request, with no usage test and one refund per account; renewals and later purchases keep the current rules. Lemon Squeezy emails customers a renewal reminder seven days before the renewal date; it is a store setting (Settings, Recovery, "Renewal reminders"), found switched off on 2026-10-15 and switched on the same day, so no reminder cron is built; the policy and FAQ state the provider’s reminder. Stated on the pricing page, in the FAQ and in the refund policy.
3. No fake scarcity. Every campaign keeps a real end date (already mandatory in `discount-campaigns.js`), is never reused under the same name, and no page shows "limited time offer", "ending soon", "prices will increase" or a struck-through "was" price. Enforced by the trust test.
4. Measurement before any price change. Landing path, referrer host and country are persisted on the checkout attempt, and a report joins attempts to verified payments by plan, offer version, campaign and landing path. Price tests are read from that report, not from GA4.
5. Free content is distribution, not a leak. No further paywall moves. The free coding workspace must be crawlable; that work is owned by the SEO roadmap, not this plan.
6. Cheap copies from GreatFrontEnd: an employer-reimbursement line with the receipt pointer on the billing success page and in the FAQ; a student code issued by hand to school addresses. Review cashback is not copied: Google's link-spam policy treats goods exchanged for links as paid links, and US FTC rules require disclosure of paid reviews.
7. Target ladder: quarterly $49 (recommended sprint), annual $99, lifetime $179 as the secondary one-time option. Rolled out as an offer version after Step 1 has data. The monthly price is open (A below).
8. Not doing: undercutting GreatFrontEnd on price, a second product, human mocks, perpetual "sale" prices.

Open, with findings recorded and the decision pending:

- A. Monthly price. $24 is below GreatFrontEnd's $39 monthly; GreatFrontEnd's "$10 a month" is the annual plan displayed per month. Monthly prices in the set: GreatFrontEnd $39, Codecademy $39.99 (per Boot.dev's comparison page), Hello Interview $47, FrontendLead $49, Boot.dev $59, BigFrontEnd $6, FrontendAtlas $12. The monthly plan exists to make the quarterly and annual look right. At $19 the quarterly saves 14% against three months and the annual 57%; at $24, 32% and 66%; at $29, 44% and 72%. Recommendation: $24, or $19 for a lower floor. In Turkey the comparison flips unless C ships, because $24 without PPP is above GreatFrontEnd's ₺760. Decided 2026-10-16: $24. Cross-sector analogs read the same way: Magoosh sells one GRE month at 83 percent of its six-month plan, Hinge+ prices the month at three times the annual rate, Brilliant and Duolingo at 1.5 to 2 times; episodic, goal-driven products carry a wide monthly spread. $24 keeps GreatFrontEnd's structure (quarterly 32 percent and annual 66 percent below three and twelve monthlies, against their 29 and 74) at 62 percent of its monthly price, and leaves $29 as the next test once the readout exists.
- B. Social-task discount. GreatFrontEnd's program: star two GitHub repos, follow LinkedIn, X and Instagram, 20% off any plan, one claim per account, "periodic validation", revocation on unfollow. Among the five paid competitors only GreatFrontEnd runs one, and no source measures its effect. GitHub's Acceptable Use Policies forbid activity "incentivized by (or incentivizes inauthentic engagement with) rewards such as cryptocurrency airdrops, tokens, credits, gifts or other give-aways" and "rank abuse, such as automated starring or following"; a discount for a star sits inside that wording. Our repo `MuslumYilmaz/uberfrontend` has 1 star. Recommendation: no star-for-discount, and no social program until there are checkouts to convert, because a coupon is a conversion lever and the constraint is traffic. If one is run later: follow or share for 10% off a first purchase, GitHub excluded, configured as a dated campaign.
- C. Purchasing-power pricing on Lemon Squeezy. Lemon Squeezy has no native country pricing; its docs point to ParityDeals, a third-party service. It is feasible without a new service: the backend runs as a Vercel Function and receives `x-vercel-ip-country`; checkout already attaches a backend-resolved `checkout[discount_code]` and hides the coupon field; Lemon Squeezy codes support product restriction, a redemption cap, an expiry and a `forever` duration for subscriptions. Design: a `countries` list on campaign entries, one tier at launch (Turkey plus a short list of low-PPP countries, 50% off quarterly and annual), codes capped and rotated quarterly, one banner line on the pricing page. A shared code can leak through the checkout URL; GreatFrontEnd's PPP carries the same risk. Recommendation: yes, after Step 1 records `countryCode` so the share of eligible visitors is known. Updated 2026-10-16: no codes after all. Lemon Squeezy's Create checkout API takes a per-checkout `custom_price` in cents that "is used for all renewal payments" on subscriptions, so the backend prices the checkout by country and nothing is created or changed in Lemon Squeezy; the regional price is shown on the page with the list price struck through and a banner line, and it never stacks with the student price (GreatFrontEnd's rule). Design in Step 7. Recommendation unchanged: yes, after Step 1 shows the country mix.

## Step 1: measurement before price changes

- Frontend: `PricingConversionContext` becomes `{ version: 2, src, surface, landingPath, referrerHost, createdAt }`, captured once per session on the first navigation (path without query, referrer host only, each capped at 200 characters, never overwritten within the session); `billing-checkout.service.ts` sends `landingPath` and `referrerHost` with the checkout start request.
- Backend: `CheckoutAttempt` gains `landingPath`, `referrerHost` (trimmed, maxlength 200, pattern-validated) and `countryCode` (two uppercase letters from `x-vercel-ip-country`, null when absent); `checkout-start.js` validates and stores them. Nothing new is sent to the provider.
- Report: `backend/scripts/report-billing-attribution.js`, read-only, grouped by ISO week, `planId`, `offerVersion`, `campaignId`, `landingPath` and `countryCode`: attempts, verified payments (`paymentVerifiedAt` set), gross `paymentTotalCents`, refunds. Run against the production URI only when the user asks.
- Tests: `billing.checkout-start.int.test.js` (fields stored, oversize or malformed values rejected, missing header yields null); a `conversion-context.service.spec.ts` (new if none exists: first touch kept, not overwritten); a unit test for the report on fixture attempts.
- Verify: `cd backend && npm test -- billing`; `cd frontend && npx ng test --watch=false --browsers=ChromeHeadlessUnit --include src/app/core/services/billing-checkout.service.spec.ts` plus the conversion-context spec.

Status (2026-10-15): implemented, committed on 2026-10-15.

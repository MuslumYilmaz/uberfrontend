# How new sites grew in 2026, and what FrontendAtlas can take from it

> Research done on 2026-10-13 into websites launched in 2026 by solo developers or tiny unfunded teams that grew fast: whether they did something in Google Search or Search Console, or already had a base, and what that means for FrontendAtlas. Five research passes covered indie launches, search-led new domains, launch platforms, channel studies, and developer-learning and interview-prep entrants. `seo-growth-roadmap-2026-10.md` tracks the on-page steps; this file is the evidence for where growth has to come from. FrontendAtlas's own numbers are in the appendix, because raw Search Console exports and the outreach registry are gitignored.

## Short version

- **Did the fast growers do something in Search Console?** No. No 2026 fast grower credits a Search Console action, and no verified 2026 case exists of a clean new domain reaching large, lasting Google traffic. Sitemaps and indexing requests change crawl timing only. Founders who published Search Console data for new domains report 1 to 7 clicks in their first one to three months.
- **Did they already have a base?** Mostly. The documented fast growers fall into three groups: a launch into the AI-agent hype wave of January to March 2026 on X, a founder with an existing audience or earlier products, or no audience and one ranking on Hacker News or GitHub trending with something free that works in seconds.
- **The only fast tactic on a new domain** was thousands of templated or AI-written pages, and Google's August and September 2026 spam updates removed it.
- **In frontend interview prep** no entrant since 2024 has verified traction. The ones still running started from a YouTube audience, and the one 2026 entrant without an audience is offline.
- **FrontendAtlas's 82 clicks in 28 days is normal** for a site with few links. Nothing in the record points to a missed setup step.
- **What FrontendAtlas can take:**
  1. Keep pull-request outreach (18 links from 99 asks). It is linear and proven.
  2. Launch one or two free, no-account, framework-neutral labs as learning pieces, on Hacker News and to Frontend Focus. The realistic prize is one to three editorial links, so judge a launch on referring domains and brand searches after six to eight weeks, not on first-week traffic.
  3. Prepare first: a Hacker News account with real history, and one return path such as an email sign-up for new labs.
  4. Skip Product Hunt, posting on X or LinkedIn from zero, more pages at scale, and forced indexing.
  5. Decide the base question separately. Every frontend-prep site still operating was built by a visible person with a following. That is a year-scale decision tied to the open byline question, and no tactic in the 2026 record replaces it.

**How to read the numbers.** Each figure carries one of four labels. *Verified* means it was read on 2026-10-13 from the platform that owns it: the GitHub or Hacker News API, a YouTube channel page, the domain registry, or payment-provider revenue shown on a TrustMRR listing (TrustMRR is a public database that reads revenue from founders' payment accounts). *Measured* means first-party analytics or a study's dataset, including FrontendAtlas's own Search Console and outreach registry. *Self-reported* means a founder's or site's own claim with no independent check. *Estimated* means a third-party tool's estimate, a count relayed by a third party, or arithmetic done here on mixed sources. Follower and subscriber counts are today's, not the counts at launch. Sources were read through an automated fetch tool, so check a figure at its source before quoting it publicly. Reddit, X, LinkedIn, Indie Hackers and Medium could not be read, so write-ups published only there are missing.

The largest headline numbers come from one event. Moltbook, RentAHuman and SimpleClaw launched within about two weeks of each other into the attention around OpenClaw, an open-source AI agent that went viral in late January, and all three spread on X ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenClaw); [andrew.ooo](https://andrew.ooo/posts/simpleclaw-18k-mrr-one-week-flip/)). This was topic timing, not a repeatable way to get users. **"Solo and unfunded" lasted only weeks in each headline case:** Meta bought Moltbook, RentAHuman entered Y Combinator ([YC Tier List](https://yctierlist.com/s26/rentahuman/)), Polsia raised $30 million (self-reported), and SimpleClaw's founder listed it for sale on day six. The big numbers are also the weakest. A security firm and several news outlets disputed Moltbook's user counts ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moltbook)), a press count relayed by a blog put RentAHuman at 82 agents against 81,000 registered people in its first week (estimated) ([Creative Collaboration](https://ccstartup.com/blog/2026/02/05/rent-a-human-wants-ai-agents-to-hire-you/)), and Polsia's own data show 84.6% of paying users leaving within 30 days (self-reported) ([SoFarBot](https://www.sofarbot.com/stories/polsia-ben-broca-500000-monthly-revenue)). Payment-verified revenue in the set runs from about $7,000 to about $380,000 over a product's life (verified); every claim above that is self-reported.

The second group started with people already listening. TrustMRR's founder, Marc Lou, had about 200,000 X followers two months before launch (estimated) and reported $20,378 in the first three days (self-reported) ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/promptway/he-built-an-app-in-24-hours-and-made-20378-the-next-day-heres-the-part-nobody-screenshots-5gep)). TrustMRR is the only case that lists search first among its channels, and it publishes one page per startup, category and tech stack ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/llms.txt)). Those pages came after a launch to a very large following, so they captured demand the audience had created. World Monitor's author runs a listed company, and the largest individual-owned 2026 repository that fronts a website (288,531 stars, verified) belongs to a developer with 49,681 GitHub followers (verified) ([GitHub](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills)). Two collections of founder stories put a number on this. An audience the founder already owned was the source of the first paying customers for 148 founders, 34%, in one (measured) ([Startup Founder Stories](https://startupfounderstories.com/data-insights/first-customers)), and for 12 of 59, 20%, in the other, where other people's communities led with 20 of 59 and search and owned content supplied 5 of 59 (measured) ([Lead Scorer](https://lead-scorer.com/blog/how-founders-get-first-customers-2026)). Both collections contain only published successes. Across 5,834 startups with verified revenue, the correlation between the founder's X followers and revenue is 0.25, and 54.2% of founders have under 1,000 followers (measured) ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/stats)). A base is the most common single starting point and it buys speed. It is not the only way in, and search rarely supplies the first users.

The third group had no audience and got one ranking on a surface that has its own ranking system. Of 18 authors of high-scoring website launches on Hacker News in 2026, 10 had never had a story above 30 points before (verified) ([Hacker News API](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?tags=show_hn&numericFilters=created_at_i%3E1767225600,points%3E300&hitsPerPage=100)). Bento, a slide deck in one HTML file, reached 1,033 points (verified) as its author's first submission ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49008211)). TorchCode and Breakscale reached thousands of GitHub stars from accounts with a few hundred followers. Your AI Slop Bores Me, built by a 17-year-old with no known earlier work, is the one from-zero site that reached tens of millions of visitors, and it did so as a joke people shared, with no monetisation mentioned. What these have in common is that the thing itself is the story, or it works within seconds, free and without an account. Two verified cases remain unexplained: Verbi and Tree Nerd Academy each passed $220,000 in verified revenue with almost no X following, and neither has a documented channel. Applied strictly (launched January to October 2026, at most three people, no outside funding, payment-verified numbers), the brief leaves three names: Verbi, a mobile app; Skillry, at $2,340 of monthly recurring revenue (verified) with a founder who has 60.1k X followers (estimated) ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/skillry)); and SimpleClaw, a one-week spike whose listing has since disappeared. **The strict list of 2026 solo successes is close to empty.**

## Search Console changed crawl timing and never traffic

Ten search-led risers had enough detail to classify. Six ran on an established domain, a famous brand or a paid link campaign, two are of unknown origin, one is a sample with no domain ages, and one claims a clean new domain on its SEO vendor's word. That one is Linkscope, a link-building marketplace: about 8,000 pages generated from two database templates, 5,210 of them indexed, and 2.11 million impressions with 5,120 clicks between 12 May and 30 June 2026, rising from about 70 to about 180 clicks a day (measured, published by the vendor) ([seoskit.com](https://seoskit.com/case-studies/programmatic-seo)). Its later traffic is known only from an Ahrefs estimate that moved from about 19,000 monthly visits to about 7,000 and then to 34,337 within seven weeks (estimated). One anonymous founder posted a graph of a site "launched in December 2025" with almost 10,000 clicks a day in January, near zero from June to September, and 9,064 clicks on 28 September (self-reported); the site, niche and method are not disclosed ([StartupTalky](https://community.startuptalky.com/discussions/post/look-at-this-google-search-console-graph-for-a-second-NjZD3Ce8zOvrR8U)). **No independently verified 2026 case exists of a clean new domain reaching large, lasting Google traffic.**

Where a base was absent, speed came from page volume, and volume is what Google removed. Google ran two core updates in 2026 and four spam updates, the spam updates on 24 March, 24 June, 18 August and 24 September ([Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history)). The August update was aimed at scaled content, thin affiliate and doorway pages, and analysts saw the September one hit heavily templated pages across countries and topics ([Glenn Gabe](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/august-2026-google-spam-update-case-studies/); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-september-2026-spam-update-weekend-impact-42174.html)). A site launched on 28 August with 720 articles in its first month lost 97% of its impressions from 3 October (self-reported) ([dev.to](https://dev.to/hisashispace/what-happened-to-my-one-month-old-site-during-googles-september-2026-spam-update-2347)). Of more than 220 sites that AI-content vendors had published as success stories, 54% later lost 30% or more of their peak organic traffic and 22% lost 75% or more (estimated from Ahrefs data), and most of the drops came after the case study was published ([Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks)). A 2026 case study of this kind is a snapshot taken near the peak.

Search Console gives an owner sitemaps for discovery, a small daily quota of recrawl requests, diagnostics and removals. Google's documentation says a sitemap does not guarantee crawling or indexing and a recrawl request does not guarantee inclusion ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/overview); [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl)), and John Mueller of Google said in February 2026 that he strongly recommends against trying to force indexing ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-force-indexing-40963.html)). The 2026 logs agree. One founder automated the Request Indexing button, had 69 URLs accepted in a day and 69% of English pages indexed within 14 days, and reports no Google clicks; three calls to the Indexing API returned success and did nothing (self-reported) ([dev.to](https://dev.to/ajiezai/from-0-to-69-indexed-in-14-days-a-new-domains-cold-start-log-2ih7)). Another submitted a sitemap on day one and after four weeks had 21.4% of 262 pages indexed and 7 clicks (self-reported) ([arnjen.com](https://arnjen.com/blog/programmatic-seo-225-pages-google-indexed-18-percent)). **No 2026 evidence exists of a Search Console action producing growth by itself.** The only actions the fast growers report are a sitemap and a few indexing requests, which the failed sites also performed. FrontendAtlas's own re-index queue of about 9 requests a day (measured on its property) gets changed pages recrawled sooner and has no evidence behind it as a ranking step.

Honest new domains are slow everywhere. In the latest Ahrefs study, 1.74% of newly published pages reached the top 10 within a year (6.11% in its second sample), and the average first-ranked page is five years old (measured) ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/)). Founders who published Search Console data for link-free new domains in 2026 report 1 click in 90 days from 33 pages, 7 clicks in four weeks from 262 pages and 5 clicks in two months from 42 pages (self-reported) ([dev.to](https://dev.to/asrocky010_7cfd07f11375df/zero-backlinks-312-impressions-what-90-days-of-search-console-looks-like-on-a-new-site-4fp9); [dev.to](https://dev.to/ritusmoikaushik/11-weeks-of-search-console-data-from-a-new-site-including-the-part-that-failed-10ig)). The author of a coding blog with more than 110 posts described the trap: Google does not index most pages of a site with no authority, authority comes from links, and nobody links to a site that does not appear in search ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47110054)). Against these logs, FrontendAtlas's 82 clicks in 28 days at an average position of 15.8 (measured) is within the normal range for a low-link site and above every log in this sample. Each ranking also yields fewer clicks than it used to. In a randomized experiment, showing AI Overviews cut organic clicks by 39.8%, and only on informational queries (measured) ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6513059)). That matches the site's own split, where trivia pages click at 0.20% and system design cases at 1.18% (measured). AI assistants do not replace the lost clicks: they sent 0.32% of visits across 101,574 sites in 2026 (measured) ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/ai-traffic-research-study/)).

## Developer-learning entrants won with free, runnable products

Seventeen developer-learning and interview-prep entrants from 2024 to 2026 were examined. **None started without an audience and grew mainly through Google.** In every case with measurable traction the first users came from a Hacker News front page, GitHub trending or Reddit. SQL Noir, a SQL detective game launched in February 2025 by a developer whose other submissions scored 1 to 4 points, got 463 points on Show HN (verified) and 30,000 visitors on its first day (self-reported) ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43041827); [HackerNoon](https://www.hackernoon.com/this-detective-game-helps-beginners-master-sql-and-database-logic)). In 2026, ShipThatCode, where learners rebuild Redis or Git against tests, reached 200 points, and Decomp Academy, where a live compiler checks the learner's code, reached 197 (verified) ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48871973); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48703412)). TorchCode has no Hacker News story at all, Breakscale's three submissions scored 2 points each, and go-interview-practice reached 2,491 stars and 1,192 forks after four Show HN posts scored 2 to 3 points each (all verified) ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/RezaSi/go-interview-practice)). Hacker News and GitHub are separate bets: a product can fail on one and grow on the other.

The entrants that grew without an audience share three properties: a skill in demand at that moment (PyTorch and LLM interviews, Go, system design), a product that does something within 30 seconds without an account, and a free or open-source core. The format is runnable practice with automatic feedback, such as a grader, a compiler, a query result or a simulation, or an interactive explainer of one system. howbrowserswork.com, a single free interactive page, reached 302 points (verified) and was linked by the TLDR developer newsletter the next day ([TLDR](https://tldr.tech/webdev/2026-01-05)); its author already ran a bootstrapped software business. Written question-and-answer banks are the format of the entrants with no verified traction.

Attention did not become a durable business where it can be checked. AlgoDrill turned a 178-point launch (verified) into $20,838 of all-time revenue, and ten months later it earns $470 a month with 3 active subscriptions (verified) ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/algodrill)). TensorTonic, the closest analogue to a practice-and-interview site, claims 50,000+ learners (self-reported) against 66 active subscriptions when its payment data was last readable in April 2026 (verified), a paid share of roughly 0.2% (estimated). Nothing found names Google as one of its channels. SQL Noir moved from free to a one-time licence a year after launch and only then added search-targeted articles, with no published result ([SQL Noir](https://www.sqlnoir.com/blog)). The free runnable core works as marketing. It is not the business.

Frontend interview prep is the weakest part of the sample. No entrant since 2024 has a verified traffic, user or revenue figure.

HelloFrontend is the closest match to FrontendAtlas's starting position: a 2026 frontend prep site with no audience. It advertised a new frontend AI track, and the topic did not carry it. FrontendLead shows the other side: its founder made videos and sold a small course first, and built the platform for customers he already had ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40212280)). The framing has a ceiling on Hacker News. Frontend interview-prep launches scored 1 to 4 points, and the best interview-prep Show HN of 2026 reached 41 points (verified) ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49643992)), while learning-framed interactive pieces reached 186 to 302 (verified). One structure looks search-first and cannot be ruled out: the company-by-role question bank. PracHub lists 499 company pages (self-reported), and Dataford, founded in 2023 and so outside the window, earns $36,645 of monthly recurring revenue (verified) with 30K+ company- and role-specific guides (self-reported) ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/dataford-inc); [Dataford](https://dataford.io/about)). Neither documents its channels, and thousands of templated pages are the pattern the 2026 spam updates removed, so this is an unverified lead and not a model.

## A front page pays a week of traffic and one to three links

Show HN, the Hacker News section for showing your own project, produced 138 posts above 300 points between 1 January and 13 October 2026 (verified), 62 of them websites (estimated, manual classification) ([Hacker News API](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?tags=show_hn&numericFilters=created_at_i%3E1767225600,points%3E300&hitsPerPage=100)). Monthly submissions ran between about 4,000 and 5,800 (measured by third parties) ([Arthur Cnops](https://www.arthurcnops.blog/death-of-show-hn/); [Zeli](https://zeli.app/story/49712005)), so roughly 0.3% of posts pass 300 points and 3 to 4% pass 30 (estimated). The website winners are games and toys, single-purpose free tools, data sites and a few interactive explainers: one idea, usable in seconds, no account, a plain title. Since spring 2026 Hacker News also refuses Show HN posts from many accounts without history. A moderator confirmed the barrier in October, and one founder whose account had karma 1 was blocked in August (self-reported) ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49938039); [dev.to](https://dev.to/leouno/our-product-hunt-launch-returned-2-upvotes-and-0-signups-here-is-every-number-89l)). First-time posters still win, from accounts that have been reading and commenting for years.

What a front page pays is poorly documented, because winners do not publish outcomes: of the 62 website authors, one gave a number. 18 Words had 132,411 players on its first day (self-reported). The only full log comes from a post that reached 69 points (verified) and peaked at rank 16: 24,222 requests on day one against 11 to 39 a day before, falling to 3,485 within the week, with Google referrals reaching 358 on day three (self-reported from server logs; these are requests, not visitors) ([intervolz.com](https://intervolz.com/sollewitt-on-hackernews/)). The lasting part is small and concrete. The top launches drew one to three named editorial pickups within 1 to 11 days (verified on the linking pages): Kottke and two other link blogs for a 1,325-point map, Frontend Focus for an 862-point font tool, TLDR for the 302-point browser guide ([Kottke](https://kottke.org/26/01/0048241-an-impressive-isometric-m); [Frontend Focus](https://frontendfoc.us/issues/760)). **No 2026 founder published a backlink count, the traffic from one newsletter mention, or traffic 30 to 90 days after a front page.** Where traffic lasted, something gave people a reason to return: news events, an ongoing social audience, or a newsletter with user submissions.

Product Hunt returns what the founder brings. It featured 4% of 162,347 launches in 2026 to date, down from 37% of 41,433 in 2023 (measured by a third-party tracker) ([hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product-hunt-pulse)). Three launches without an audience report 2, 4 and 6 upvotes and no sign-ups or paying users (self-reported) ([dev.to](https://dev.to/leouno/our-product-hunt-launch-returned-2-upvotes-and-0-signups-here-is-every-number-89l); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49691289); [dev.to](https://dev.to/aritomofukuda/2-weeks-after-product-hunt-the-numbers-nobody-shows-you-1m2b)). The best solo result found, 517 votes and 2,021 registrations in 22 days, followed eight months of building in public (self-reported) ([own.page](https://own.page/blog/how-to-launch-on-product-hunt)). GitHub rewards a repository that is itself the product. The browser guide got 210 stars from its 302-point launch, and TorchCode got 4,665 with no Hacker News story (verified). The 378 repositories created in 2026 with more than 8,000 stars (verified) are dominated by add-ons for AI coding agents, and of the 16 interview-related repositories created in 2026 with more than 1,000 stars, none is a frontend or JavaScript resource (verified) ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=interview+created:%3E2026-01-01+stars:%3E1000&sort=stars&order=desc)). The two AI-engineering interview lists among them were published by educators with 6,301 and 8,881 GitHub followers (verified), so a new topic alone did not carry a question list. Developers themselves use GitHub (69.5%), YouTube (58.4%) and Reddit (53.8%) far more than Hacker News (19.6%) or X (17.1%) (measured, Stack Overflow surveys of 2026 and 2025) ([Stack Overflow 2026](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/community); [Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology)).

## FrontendAtlas has the winning format but has not launched it

FrontendAtlas's position, from its own Search Console and outreach registry on 2026-10-13 (all measured): 82 clicks in 28 days, about 6 editorial links among 104 links from 42 sites, effectively no brand searches, and 517 outreach asks that produced 24 links, 18 of them from 99 pull requests (18%) and 3 from 231 emails (1.3%). That is the profile of a clean domain with no base, and the cases say what such a site can and cannot expect. It cannot expect a Search Console step or a larger page count to change the curve. It can keep adding links one at a time by pull request, which works, and it can attempt the one fast route the record shows for people with no audience.

That route fits what the site already builds. The winners without an audience shipped runnable practice and interactive explainers, and FrontendAtlas has a coding workspace with tests and six interactive labs. Its own data show the labs do not work through search, with 18 clicks from 12,789 impressions in three months (measured), and do work through people: the CSS sticky debugging lab, which is framework-neutral, is the one that earned links from caniuse.com and the Frontend Dogma newsletter. What is missing is a launch. The outreach record lists pull requests, emails, forms, issues and discussions. Community posts on Reddit and Hashnode were screened in September 2026 and deferred or excluded. The only Hacker News attempt was a Show HN for the whole site on 2026-02-24 under an interview-prep title; it has 1 point and no comments and is absent from the public Hacker News search index. The account that posted it has karma 1 and no comments, so it meets the barrier for accounts without history. The cases set the conditions for one: a single piece that works on a phone within 30 seconds with no account and no paywall, on a skill in demand now, presented as a way to understand something and not as interview prep, posted under a plain title from a Hacker News account with real history, and sent directly to Frontend Focus, whose four sampled issues each linked solo-built tools or explainer sites ([Frontend Focus](https://frontendfoc.us/issues/761)). The existing framework-neutral labs (JavaScript event loop, CSS sticky) are ready candidates, and the planned stream, stop and retry lab for AI-product interfaces fits the in-demand-skill condition. This fit is an analogy from other people's launches, not evidence about this site, and the topic will not carry a launch by itself, as HelloFrontend's AI track shows.

The honest expectation is modest. About 3 to 4% of Show HN posts pass 30 points (estimated), and a clear success pays a week of traffic and one to three editorial links, about what 6 to 17 pull requests produce at the measured 18% rate (estimated, arithmetic on the two sets of figures). The difference is that these links are unasked and come from publications developers read, which is the kind of link the site has about 6 of. A launch therefore belongs in the authority budget, not the traffic budget, and should be judged six to eight weeks later on new referring domains and brand searches. The spike fades within a week unless visitors have a reason to return, so one simple return path, such as an email sign-up for new labs, should exist before launch. A launch will not fix revenue either: the comparable sites kept the launched piece free, and paid conversion where it can be checked was about 0.2% (estimated).

The larger finding concerns the base. Every frontend interview-prep entrant still operating was built by a person with a following first, and FrontendLead suggests the following can be small if it matches intent: a channel with 7.12K subscribers today (verified) and 1,000+ paying course customers before the platform existed (self-reported). Channels started after the product do nothing: TensorTonic's has 1 subscriber and PracHub's has 26 (verified) ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@TensorTonic/about); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@Prachubdotcom/about)). A base is a decision on the scale of a year and it depends on a visible person, which is the open byline question for this site. No tactic in the 2026 record substitutes for it. Search remains worth the on-page work already done, as the channel that pays after authority arrives: it supplied only 8 to 9% of first customers in the founder collections (measured), yet in the same success-only collection founders report search and content among the channels that work later in a product's life ([Startup Founder Stories](https://startupfounderstories.com/data-insights/marketing-channels)).

| Route | What the 2026 record shows | Verdict for FrontendAtlas |
|---|---|---|
| Search Console actions (sitemaps, indexing requests) | Affect discovery and crawl timing only; no growth case | Keep as hygiene; expect nothing more |
| Many more pages (templated or AI-written) | The only fast tactic on a new domain; removed by the August and September spam updates | Do not use |
| Pull requests that add an exact-fit page | 18 links from 99 asks on this site (measured) | Continue; linear and proven |
| Show HN with a free interactive lab | Learning-framed explainers scored 186 to 302 points; interview-prep framing peaked at 41 (verified) | A low-odds bet worth taking once or twice; needs an account with history |
| Frontend newsletters | Pickups follow Hacker News hits; Frontend Focus also links solo-built tools; 1 link from 14 asks on this site (measured) | Pitch only with a framework-neutral lab |
| GitHub repository | Stars come when the repository is the runnable product; no frontend interview repository from 2026 passed 1,000 stars (verified) | Only as runnable challenges with tests, never a question list |
| Product Hunt | 2 to 6 upvotes without an audience (self-reported) | Skip |
| Posting on X or LinkedIn from zero | X is used by 17.1% of developers (measured); the fast X cases had a following or a hype wave | Skip as a launch channel |
| YouTube or another creator base | The only origin of the surviving frontend-prep sites; takes a year or more | A strategic decision, not a tactic |
| Reddit | Named in the SQL Noir and TensorTonic stories; largest first-customer source in one collection, 15 of 59 (measured) | Not evaluated: the research tools could not read it |

## Conclusion

The question offered two explanations, a Search Console technique or an existing base. The evidence closes the first and adds a third beside the second: one ranking on a community surface. It is the only route to fast attention that the 2026 record shows working for people with no audience, and it works for a thing people can use at once, not for a site as a whole. That changes the choice in front of FrontendAtlas. The choice is not between more search work and a growth trick. It is between three things that can run together: linear link-building that already converts, a few low-odds launches of free standalone labs whose realistic prize is editorial links, and the year-scale decision to build a base around a visible person. Only the third has produced a frontend interview-prep business in this sample.

The assessment would change with evidence this research could not reach: Reddit threads, where SQL Noir and TensorTonic appear to have started; traffic estimates for PracHub, the one entrant whose structure looks search-first; and any 2026 founder publishing Search Console data for a clean domain that grew. Winners rarely publish outcomes, so the record understates both quiet successes and quiet failures. A first standalone launch by FrontendAtlas would itself be new evidence, provided it is judged on referring domains and brand searches and not on the first week's traffic.

## Addendum: what Google lifts for a zero-start site (researched 2026-10-13, written 2026-10-14)

A follow-up pass asked a narrower question than the sections above: what Google's systems reward on a site with no history, whatever the niche.

- Authority sets the ceiling. Ahrefs' June 2026 Search Console benchmark of 344,956 sites puts median monthly clicks at 9 for DR 0 to 10, 60 for DR 10 to 20, 133 for DR 20 to 30, 308 for DR 30 to 40, 831 for DR 40 to 50 and 1,594 for DR 50 to 60. FrontendAtlas' 82 clicks in 28 days sits between the DR 10 to 20 and DR 20 to 30 medians, which fits its real authority once off-topic links are discounted. The gap is authority, not a penalty.
- New pages rarely break through, and the ones that do move fast. In Ahrefs' May 2025 study, 1.74% of new pages reached the top 10 within a year (5.7% in 2017), 40.82% of those did it within a month, and 72.9% of top-10 pages were older than three years.
- Secondary write-ups of the DOJ trial exhibits describe ranking around anchors, body and clicks, a trust signal that treats PageRank as distance from known good sources, popularity from Chrome visits and anchors, and Navboost click memory over 13 months. Click memory favors incumbents, so a query with no click history is where a new page gets a fair test.
- Cyrus Shepard's 400-site study found the strongest features of winning sites were a product or service (correlation 0.391), task completion (0.381) and proprietary assets (0.357), ahead of topical focus (0.250) and brand (0.206). Sites with four or five of these features won about 68 to 70% of the time, against 13.5% for the rest.
- The March 2026 core update lifted originators over aggregators (Lily Ray's analysis), and the August 2026 spam update pushed 16.71% of top-10 URLs beyond position 100.

Consequence: a page that FrontendAtlas repositions, or adds when the owner asks for one, should target a query with no click history yet or let the reader finish a task on the page. The first application is the Angular 22 wave, which repositions two existing pages and is tracked as Step 3c in `docs/references/seo-growth-roadmap-2026-10.md`.

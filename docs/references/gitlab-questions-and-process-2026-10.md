# GitLab: reported frontend interview questions and interview process

> Working record started on 2026-10-23. It lists the frontend interview questions and tasks that public interview reports attribute to GitLab, with a source and access status for every item, and the interview stages that those reports and GitLab's own handbook describe. Background on why GitLab was chosen: `reports/company-candidates-research-2026-10-23.md`. Format: `docs/references/shopee-questions-and-process-2026-10.md` (its "Why", "Implementation" and "Status" sections are left out here). The user approved GitLab for the next company wave on 2026-10-23; the build is recorded under Implementation.

## Why GitLab (2026-10-23)

Chosen in the candidate round recorded in `company-pages-plan-2026-10.md` (Candidate selection rule) and approved by the user on 2026-10-23 together with Anduril and Capital One.

- **Not covered.** No GreatFrontEnd company guide and no Front End Interview Handbook company page (lists re-read on 2026-10-22).
- **Demand.** Google suggestions on 2026-10-22: `gitlab frontend engineer interview`, `gitlab frontend interview`, `gitlab interview process`; Bing returned seven.
- **Hiring on the day.** Two frontend-titled postings (Senior and Staff Frontend Engineer) on 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-23. Both are listed in Bangalore, India, first published 2026-09-24; the candidate report had called them remote.
- **Readable sources.** GitLab publishes the whole frontend loop; 41 Taro frontend or full-stack reports agree with it from 2018, plus independent Medium and Blind accounts.
- **Buyers.** Remote roles worldwide with location-based eligibility; the current frontend openings are for candidates in India, which the selection rule treats as a separate lane. The user approved the company knowing the hiring signal.

## Research rules

- A question counts only if it is on a page our tools read in full (F) or appears verbatim in a search snippet (S). Unreadable sources are marked U. Every source cited below is F except GLB13 (U, listed so it is not re-checked). No snippet-only item is used.
- Glassdoor, 1point3acres, Prepfully, Dataford, PracHub and templated "N questions" pages are not cited. Several discovery searches returned only Glassdoor review pages; none was opened.
- Taro (jointaro.com) interview pages are Glassdoor reviews re-hosted with rewritten text, and Taro can change the city and date. Only the public pages were read: the page HTML with curl, the "Related GitLab Interview Experiences" links on those pages, and Taro's public sitemap (`/interview-experiences-sitemap.xml`), which lists exactly 205 GitLab experience URLs, the same number the pages show ("View All 205 Experiences"). No Taro API was called. Each report is counted once: a word-shingle comparison of all 205 pages found no duplicate frontend report. A Taro date on the 1st of a month is a placeholder and is written `YYYY-MM*`.
- Blind: every comment was read (the comment counts in the page data match what was read). Each claim is marked as the original poster (OP), a commenter (c) or a plan written before the interview (p), with the company badge.
- Medium was read through the author RSS feed, dev.to through its public article API, LeetCode through the GraphQL recipe (keyword search, topic, comments).
- Quotes are the exact words on the page, in quotation marks. Taro quotes are Taro's rewritten text. Anything not in quotation marks is a paraphrase.
- Exclusions: design, marketing-site and infrastructure roles appear only as contrast. GitHub is a different company and was not used.

## Method

One research agent worked on 2026-10-23, read-only on the repository.

- Official pages, read in full with curl: the frontend job family page, the Associate, Intermediate, Senior and Staff level pages, the technical interview page, the candidate handbook page, "Conducting a GitLab Interview" and the hiring index. Also read: the AI interview guidelines page and the 2019 GitLab blog post, both linked from the technical page, the frontend development guide on docs.gitlab.com, and GitLab's public Greenhouse board API (231 postings) with three job posts.
- Candidate reports: the six Taro URLs in the brief, then every frontend-titled or full-stack-titled GitLab URL in Taro's sitemap (41 pages, all read), then a scan of the other 164 GitLab Taro pages for frontend terms (5 hits, all read, 3 with frontend content), plus one Taro work review found by search. Medium (six author feeds, four posts used), dev.to (2 posts), Blind (4 threads), LeetCode (a "gitlab" keyword search returned 30 posts; the 2 GitLab threads were read with their comments).
- WebSearch: 10 calls (Taro twice, Blind twice, LeetCode, Medium twice, dev.to twice, one open query). New finds: GL7, GL30, GL19, GL44, GLB3, GLB8, GLB11, GLB12, GLB13. The other 24 frontend Taro pages came from the sitemap, not from search.
- Raw copies of every page read were kept for the session (229 HTML pages plus feeds, JSON and text conversions); they are not part of the repository.

## Verdict

- Depth: strong on format, thin on recency. GitLab publishes its full frontend loop (O1 to O7), and 43 Taro pages from 2016 to 2025 (41 frontend or full-stack interview reports, one senior software engineer report with a frontend round, one employee review that describes the interviews) agree with it from 2018 onward. The technical round is a merge request (MR) on a small Vue app: the candidate reviews it in writing before the call, then improves it in a 90-minute live session. But only five Taro reports date from 2023 or later (GL1 to GL5), and only two of them describe the MR in any detail (GL3, GL4, both 2024). Independent first-party frontend accounts are few: a full-stack Medium post with a separate frontend technical round (GL44, interviews in early 2025, offer), a 2018 Medium post that ended at the recruiter call (GL46), and a 2024 Blind thread whose role is not stated (GL45). Backend accounts (GLB1 to GLB3) confirm the same MR format.
- Formats by era: 2016 to 2017, a screening call, a JavaScript, DOM and CSS question round, then calls up to the CEO (GL39 to GL43). From 2018, an application questionnaire with written technical questions, a recruiter screen, the MR review plus live implementation, then manager and director interviews (GL35, GL27, GL24, GL20, GL21, GL28, O8). One 2019 loop still used an hour of JavaScript questions and a 10-minute linked-list exercise (GL34). The MR was still a PR "in GitLab's codebase" in 2020 (GL16); the handbook now says it is self-contained and "drawn from a curated bank of technical exercises" (O4). Since 2020 some candidates met the hiring manager or a director before the technical round (GL9, GL5, GL2); full-stack candidates get separate backend and frontend technical rounds (GL18, GL19, GL7, GL44). The 2026 official text adds AI use with disclosure (O4, O6) and camera-on interviews after the recruiter screen (O7).
- Where reports come from: United States 18, United Kingdom 4, Canada 3, India 3, Netherlands 2, Paraguay 2, Australia 2, and one each from Ireland, Germany, Switzerland, Turkey, Greece, Cyprus, Israel, Norway and Russia (Taro locations, which can be altered). Levels: mostly "Frontend Engineer" with no level; senior titles in GL9, GL11, GL19, GL25, GL30, GL31, GL33; full-stack in GL5, GL7, GL14, GL18, GL19, GL29, GL44; no Staff account. 12 reports ended in an offer (GL14, GL18, GL19, GL20, GL21, GL23, GL24, GL28, GL30, GL35, GL43, GL44); GL39 is labelled "Got Offer" but its text says "The process is ongoing for me".
- Newest reports: GL1 (India, 2025-10-18, recruiter round only), GL2 (Canada, 2025-07*, rejected after the hiring-manager interview), GL44 (Medium, full-stack, interviews early 2025, offer), GL4 and GL3 (2024-02* and 2024-03*, the newest MR detail), GLB9 (LeetCode, 2026-09, a plan for an India senior software engineer loop, not frontend).
- Titles and level names: GitLab's own titles are Intern, Associate, Intermediate, Senior and Staff Frontend Engineer (O1). Candidates write "Front End Engineer" (GL3), "Frontend Developer" (GL1), "Senior Frontend Developer" (GL9), "Senior JavaScript Engineer" (GL25), "Fronted Engineer" (sic, GL12) and "Full Stack Engineer" (GL7). Two current postings are "Senior Frontend Engineer" and "Staff Frontend Engineer" (O10).
- Stack, only as the sources state it: Vue ("Professional experience with VueJS or another modern JavaScript web framework", O1; "a simple Vue 2 app with an API call", GL3; "done in Vue.js along with unit tests in Jest", GL4); Jest (O1 lists it as an example of automated tests; GL4); Rails and Haml ("Working knowledge of Ruby on Rails and Haml" is a nice-to-have in O1; "GitLab is built on top of Ruby on Rails. It uses Haml and a JavaScript-based frontend with Vue.js.", O9; GL16 saw a Haml page with a Vue instance); GraphQL ("When making API calls, we use GraphQL as the first choice.", O9; listed as "Helpful, but not required" in the Senior posting, O10; no candidate report mentions GraphQL); Vuex and VueX (GL35's 2018 written question; O1's Software Supply Chain Security specialty; O9 now says "Vuex is deprecated"); ECharts (O1, Monitor specialty).
- Hiring signal, 2026-10-23 (O10): 231 postings on GitLab's Greenhouse board; two are frontend-titled, "Senior Frontend Engineer" and "Staff Frontend Engineer", both with location "Bangalore, India" and office "India", first published 2026-09-24, on the Core DevOps team that owns "core repository capabilities". Three full-stack postings are remote in Canada or the United States, one of them "Staff Fullstack Engineer (Ruby/Vue.js) Monetization Engineering: Purchase". The background report called the two frontend roles remote; the posts say "All of our roles are remote, however some roles may carry specific location-based eligibility requirements." and the board lists them under India, so the open frontend roles today are for candidates in India.
- What ranks: NOT MEASURED for "gitlab frontend interview questions" in this pass (the search budget went to source discovery). In the discovery searches, Glassdoor review pages filled most results, Taro pages and GitLab's own job family pages also appeared, and a Dataford template page for GitLab frontend engineers appeared (not opened, not evidence).

## Loop by level and region

GitLab is all-remote and publishes one loop for every frontend level, so this section is organised by the official loop, then by era, role and level, then by region and logistics.

### Official loop, all levels (read 2026-10-23)

- Job family page (O1): "Selected candidates will be invited to complete a short written assessment." → "Next, candidates will be invited to schedule a 30 minute screening call with our Global Recruiters" → "Next, candidates will be invited to schedule a 90 minute technical interview with either a Frontend Engineer or a Frontend Hiring Manager" → "Next, candidates will be invited to schedule a 45 minute behavioral interview with a Frontend Hiring Manager" → "Candidates will then be invited to schedule a 60 minute interview with a Director of Engineering" → "Successful candidates will subsequently be made an offer." It adds: "Please keep in mind that candidates can be declined from the position at any stage of the process."
- Level pages (O2, O3, and the Associate and Intermediate pages): the same six steps, with the technical interview "with either a Frontend Engineer or a Frontend Engineering Manager" and the behavioral interview "with an Engineering Manager". No level page adds a round or changes a duration.
- Technical interview (O4): the merge request arrives "at least 72 hours before your scheduled call"; "We encourage you to dedicate up to an hour to review it"; "please complete your review at least 24 hours prior to the scheduled time"; then "a 90-minute video call and screen-sharing session with the interviewer(s), where they will ask you to walk them through your review and where you will also write some code to improve the merge request."
- Candidate handbook (O5): stages "Application Process", "Screening Call", "Assessment (required for certain roles)", "Technical Interview (required for certain roles)", "Team Interviews", "References & TMRG Connection", "Offer & Background Screening". Screening calls "can last anywhere between 30 and 40 minutes"; "Team Interviews may consist of behavioral, panel and/or technical interviews (certain positions)."
- Interviewer guide (O7): "All interviews following a recruiter screen should take place on camera for both the candidate and interviewer." Engineering scorecards have a level category: "the interviewer can score the candidate on multiple levels (ex. the Senior level and the Intermediate level)."

## Proposed interview process tables

Registry rule: a stage cites only sources read in full. Every source below is F. GitLab's own pages carry each stage, and candidate reports confirm it. Two tables are supported; the senior theory round and the India loop are left as gaps.

**Variant 1: front-end engineers, all levels, remote (official 2026 loop; candidate reports 2018 to 2025 agree)**

| Stage | What happens | Who | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Written assessment | A short written assessment after applying; reports describe open-ended technical questions answered in writing, such as what happens in the browser while a page loads | Selected applicants | O1; GL35, GL27, GL24, GL6 |
| Recruiter screening call | 30 to 40 minutes on video: background, motivation, experience with each listed skill, location, notice period, salary range | All | O1, O5; GL4, GL1, GL32 |
| Merge request review (on your own time) | A link to a self-contained merge request on a small app at least 72 hours before the call; up to an hour of review; finish 24 hours before and leave a note; reports name Vue, HTML, CSS, Haml and Jest tests | Front-end engineers | O4; GL3, GL4, GL10, GL15, GL16 |
| Technical interview | 90-minute video call with screen sharing: walk through the review, then write code to improve the merge request; a frontend engineer or frontend engineering manager runs it | Front-end engineers | O1, O2, O4; GL4, GL21, GL23, GL28 |
| Behavioural interview | 45 minutes with an engineering manager; STAR questions, sometimes high-level technical questions | All | O1, O2, O7; GL20, GL28, GL21 |
| Director of Engineering interview | 60 minutes; past experience and values, with a few technical questions | All | O1; GL20, GL21, GL28 |
| References, background check and offer | Three references, at least one a past manager; a background screen depending on location; then the offer | Candidates who pass | O5; GL21, GL18 |

Caveats for the table text: the order can change (GL9, GL5 and GL2 met a manager or director before the technical round); a role can close mid-loop and the candidate is moved to another team (GL35, GL9); AI use is welcome in the technical assessment if disclosed (O6).

## Practice prompts (proposal)

Each prompt is modelled on a reported round or on the official merge-request format. Linking a prompt to a pinned item needs no re-pin.

1. **Review a small Vue merge request, then improve it live.** You get a merge request on a small Vue app that fetches a list, transforms it and renders it conditionally; review it within an hour, finish a day before the call, then walk through your comments and code the most important fixes in 90 minutes. Modelled on O4 (format) and GL3 and GL4 (the app). Links to `vue-debounced-search` (PINNED, link only).
2. **Stop the component from refetching on every click.** The merge request calls the same endpoint each time a button is clicked; explain the cost in a review comment, then change it so the data loads once and is reused while loading and error states stay correct. Modelled on GL4's hindsight and GL3's "caching data in the component and reduced unnecessary API calls". Links to `js-debug-async-race`.
3. **Fix the markup and match the mobile mock.** The merge request has invalid HTML and a view that breaks on narrow screens; correct the markup, then use Flexbox and media queries so the layout matches a mobile mock, and justify each change in a comment. Modelled on GL16 (broken HTML), GL3 and GL4 (Flexbox, media queries, the mobile mock). Links to `css-flexbox-navbar`.
4. **Make the unit tests catch the bug.** The merge request changes a component's data-loading method and its unit test still passes; write a spy-based test that fails on the bug and passes on the fix, then say what the test protects. Modelled on GL4 ("unit tests in Jest"), GL16 (failing unit tests and CI) and O1 ("Frontend Unit Test Coverage"). Links to `js-create-spy-function`.
5. **Answer the written question on page loading.** In writing, describe what happens in the browser from the request to a painted, interactive page, in as much detail as you think is useful, and name one performance risk at each step. Modelled on the written assessment in GL35 and GL27 and the O1 requirement on how the browser parses and constructs a page. Links to `html-parsing-rendering`.

Reserve prompt: **Speed up a slow-loading list, then add a tagged search box** (GL30, Senior, 2019), linking to `infinite-scroll-list` and `vue-chips-input-autocomplete` (PINNED, link only).

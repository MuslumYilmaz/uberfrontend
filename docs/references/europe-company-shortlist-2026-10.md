## Decisions recorded on 2026-10-16

- GreatFrontEnd's company guides cover US big tech only (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Amazon, Apple, Stripe, Figma, Databricks, Ramp and others). Non-US companies are a gap in editorial guides, but the gap is filled by templated pages (Dataford, Prepfully, Aced, cleverprep). The differentiator is therefore not geography on its own; it is the same rule as the US pages: every prompt cites a public interview report.
- Region: Europe before India. India has the larger volume but India-priced, user-generated competitors (GeeksforGeeks, Code360, LearnersBucket, frontendgeek) rank above us on an Indian SERP, and willingness to pay is lower. Europe has weaker template competition and a higher price ceiling. Turkey is out: eight Turkish-language company queries returned no company-plus-frontend result (market research, section 4).
- Scope: three or four companies chosen by search demand first, then filtered by whether public interview reports with concrete question content exist. No pages are written until the shortlist is confirmed and a separate yes/no is given for writing them.
- "Adding a tag" means adding a company slug to `companies: string[]` on an existing catalog question; a company gets a prerender route, a sitemap entry and a directory card only when at least one tagged question exists. This is on hold until the companies are chosen.

## Shortlist

1. Revolut: highest demand, good source depth.
2. Booking.com: good sources, and the Angular and RxJS side overlaps the catalog.
3. Adyen: longest source trail; the take-home format fits the worked-guide style.
4. Zalando or Spotify: demand exists; the source research decides which one. A company with demand but hard-to-find sources is attractive because the competition is thinner there.

Reserve: Wise, Delivery Hero, Miro (Miro is already marked open and sourced in the market research).

## Source research

Done on 2026-10-16: six research agents (one per company, about 600 tool calls in total), then six spot checks by the coordinating session (Zalando LeetCode post through GraphQL, Booking.com Code360 JSON, Adyen take-home README, Revolut official Medium post through the feed, Revolut Taro page, Spotify Blind thread), all of which matched the agents' claims. Rules: a source counts only if it was read by our tools or appears verbatim in a search result; access status is recorded per source (F = read in full, S = snippet only, U = unreadable). Glassdoor and 1point3acres are snippet-only everywhere; Reddit was not attempted. Only sources with frontend-relevant content are tabled; backend, mobile, QA and ML reports were read for loop shape and are summarised in prose.

Cross-company warnings:

- Taro "experiences" for Revolut and Booking.com are Glassdoor reviews re-hosted with different city and date metadata (Revolut: Taro "Warsaw 2025-04" is Glassdoor "Dubai 2026-01"; Taro "Barcelona 2025-05" is Glassdoor "Portugal 2026-02"). Count each report once and trust the wording, not the location.
- Prepfully pages that claim "N questions verified by <Company> Frontend Engineers" are a template bank: Zalando's `__NEXT_DATA__` flags 16 of 20 inspected items `is_generic: true`, each attributed to 6 to 400 companies. Dataford pages (403 or 429 to us) are templated too; its Zalando frontend guide says 6 rounds while its full-stack sibling says 3.
- Misattributions caught by reading the page: the Blind thread `reddit-frontend-interview-jtjrhkzh` (XSS, REST, lodash-style functions) is Reddit's loop, Adyen is only a commenter's tag; the Blind "Interview at Spotify Sweden" HTML/CSS/JS-trivia plus FizzBuzz description is a WeWork commenter about another company; GitHub repos titled "Spotify Web Engineer Challenge" are Shopify's Toronto Waste Wizard challenge; the search summary's "front-end interview question" for Taro's Spotify NY 2025-02 report is not on the page.

### Revolut

Verdict: the strongest file. About 12 distinct frontend reports from 2020 to 2026 (Glassdoor snippets, Taro full reads, two Blind threads), one official Revolut Tech post from 2025-10 stating the interview philosophy, and a verbatim historical take-home brief in candidate repositories. Who ranks for `revolut frontend interview`: Glassdoor (three title variants), two Taro mirrors, Dataford, OphyAI, one Blind thread; only Glassdoor and Taro carry candidate content. Official title is "Software Engineer (Frontend)" (also "Graduate Programme 2027: Software Engineer (Frontend)" in Barcelona, Dubai, Krakow, Lisbon, London, Madrid); "Web Engineer" is not a title. Open count is unverifiable (every revolut.com careers URL answers a bot check); search snippets show five distinct frontend postings.

Loop (2023 to 2026 reports): recruiter call; then either a HackerRank JS test (3 tasks, 85 minutes; "sliding window", one "needing graph traversal", third task "high-medium LeetCode") or a 15-minute theory check run by the recruiter from a checklist (React reconciliation, micro- versus macrotasks, "what is the DOM tree"); then one hour of live coding in TypeScript and React with boilerplate provided, the best-documented task being fetch data from a source and render it (loading state, error handling, types; optimisation or unit tests if time permits; rejection feedback quoted "type management and loading-state handling"), with variants "pairing session using a service API with authentication to display data in a React component", "improve a small React app", and a senior complaint about requirements changing mid-task; then, in 2025 to 2026 reports only, frontend system design ("suggest product features and design the APIs between the frontend and other services"; one senior prompt "design Revolut's physical card delivery system: API, frontend, overall system"); then team fit and a Bar Raiser values round. JS theory named across reports: closures, `this`, timers, type coercion, promises, call stack, "which JavaScript methods are mutable", predict-the-console-output questions; React lifecycle, Redux, reconciliation, rendering flow.

Historical take-home (2016 to 2022, not reported since): "implement the exchange screen of the Revolut app as a web widget, poll ECB or openexchangerates every 10 seconds for GBP, EUR, USD, React (Preact) and Redux", verbatim in two 2017 repositories, about 15 public solutions, last dated 2021-12. Unverified: "design a real-time transaction feed" and "Virtual DOM" exist only in Dataford's template; `shoaibshebi/revolut-interview-prep` (2026-09) describes a "Round 2 roadmap" of unknown provenance.

### Zalando

Verdict: one full first-hand frontend report with concrete questions (LeetCode, Senior Frontend, Berlin, 2024-07, hired; republished by the same author on Medium in 2024-09), Codility task texts surviving on GitHub (2018 to 2019) and corroborated by a Glassdoor question snippet, an official five-stage loop page plus a 2023 blog post on the technical interview, and dense Glassdoor snippets from 2016 to 2025. German-language sources hold no frontend content (Kununu answers 405 "Human Verification"; glassdoor.de mirrors the English reports). Who ranks for `zalando frontend interview questions`: two Glassdoor pages, Dataford, seven Prepfully pages; nothing first-hand ranks. Open roles (jobs.zalando.com sitemap, 173 live postings): category "Software Engineering - Frontend" has 2 (Principal Frontend Engineer, ZEOS Portal, Berlin; Senior Fullstack Engineer, ZEOS, Berlin) plus 4 React full-stack roles in Berlin and Dublin; postings indexed last week for Marketing Platform, Loyalty and "React + ReactNative" are now 404.

Loop: TA partner call 30 minutes; Codility take-home (frontend candidates report 2 to 3 hours; 2021 and 2024: "React-Node application: a search box with an auto-suggestion list on every keystroke", with debounce and unit tests; 2023: 135 minutes, 3 tasks); hiring manager 30 to 60 minutes; final loop of 3 to 5 interviews of 45 to 60 minutes: solution discussion or live coding, JavaScript and web fundamentals, system design (one hour), engineering manager. Official timeline 2 to 3 weeks; candidates report longer waits and ghosting.

Concrete items from the 2024 report: "what happens if we removed the imports", "how can you optimise the application further", "write your own debounce"; async versus defer, CSSOM, FCP and TTI, SSR, blocking versus non-blocking loading; CSS specificity, HTML5 semantics, ARIA roles, `aria-hidden`, `aria-labelledby`, `aria-describedby`, VoiceOver, sprites versus SVG; types and prototypes; ES6 map, destructuring, filter, some; Builder pattern, SOLID; request headers, cookies, clickjacking, XSS; webpack minification and bundling; system design with a load balancer and database read/write optimisation; manager question "what will you do immediately if your code breaks production". Glassdoor snippets add: implement a memoize function, convert a class component to a functional one and refactor React components (2023), "implement a menu popup on hover" in JSFiddle, the event loop in browser and Node, `requestAnimationFrame`, CSS versus JS animations, flatten arrays, cancelable promises, media queries and pseudo-elements, system design of "a shopping cart page and widget" (2024), hydration, PWA, CDN, DNS and browser caching. The 2018 to 2019 Codility screen was a jQuery comments widget (GET `/comments?count=N` per `.comment-list[data-count]`) plus a Promise function returning N unique liked brands or rejecting with `CustomError("Not enough data")`; a Codility library task also seen at LINE, likely retired.

### Spotify

Verdict: no Europe-located, dated web-engineer report with verbatim questions exists. Six independent self-reports (Blind 2021 to 2022, Glassdoor 2022 to 2026 snippets, 1point3acres about 2021, a 2018 intern post) agree on a four-round final and give about ten concrete tasks; most candidates were in the US, Stockholm applicants describe the same shape, and interviewing.io notes hiring is centralised. Official: `lifeatspotify.com/how-we-hire` and `/how-we-hire/interview` (read in full) give the stages, the tools (CoderPad and Mural) and the values criteria, with no technical content; the engineering blog has no post on the engineering loop. No web take-home appears in any report; the practical round is live in CoderPad. Open roles (Lever feed, 81 postings): web-titled 4, Senior Web Engineer, Subscriptions (London; TypeScript, React, Next.js, GraphQL), Senior Fullstack Engineer (London and Stockholm, Platform), Senior Fullstack Engineer, Messaging Platform (New York). Titles: Web Engineer, Senior Web Engineer, Frontend Engineer, Fullstack Engineer. Who ranks: Dataford (template; it is the sole origin of a "modular dashboard with real-time state updates" prompt), Glassdoor review pages, two thin Blind threads, educative, knok.work and Prepfully templates. Nobody ranking has sourced web content.

Loop: recruiter call 30 minutes (why leaving, why Spotify, current stack); tech screen 60 to 90 minutes with one or two engineers (recent project, "what would you change", "how do you handle scale"; web trivia: box model, specificity, `setTimeout` versus Promise versus `bind` output ordering, closures, hoisting, `this`, memoization; one easy-to-medium coding problem: "write a closure that limits how many times a function can be called", remove duplicates preserving order, a sliding-window problem); final round of four 60-minute sessions: data structures and algorithms (LeetCode easy to medium, "different from standard LeetCode"), the web practical in CoderPad, described by a recruiter as "JavaScript, DOM, CSS fundamentals deep-dive as well as application of web native APIs" (reported tasks: build a form that fetches from the Spotify API and renders results into the DOM; build an image carousel; a traffic light in HTML, CSS and JS that blinks with animations; recreate a two-button mockup in HTML and CSS; one 2022 candidate wrote no CSS), system design ("a product like Twitter"; "Instagram profile page: implementation, infinite scroll, debounce versus throttle, API design"), and values (STAR). Timeline 3 weeks to 3 months.

### Booking.com

Verdict: a consistent frontend loop from 2016 to 2025 with recurring tasks verified across four or more independent reports, several of them readable in full (Code360 and enginebogie JSON APIs, Blind, Taro, dev.to), and dense Glassdoor snippets. Two caveats. First, concrete frontend reports stop in mid-2023; the 2025 to 2026 reports are general software-engineer loops (HackerRank, role-specific take-home, pairing with two engineers to explain, debug, extend and test it, system design, culture fit). Second, hiring: jobs.booking.com lists 134 open roles and zero frontend-titled roles in Amsterdam, Manchester, Bangalore or Shanghai; the only one is "Frontend Software Engineer - Pipeline" in Bucharest (req 29899, 2026-07). The Angular and RxJS hint from the market research rests on a single 2022 Amsterdam report with no coding task; every official artefact (graduate programme posts, 2027 internship JD, Romania JD) says React, so the catalog-overlap argument for Booking.com is withdrawn. Who ranks for `booking.com frontend interview`: Code360, Blind, Glassdoor, Taro and enginebogie (all with concrete content) against jobrise, techinterview.org and Prepfully templates; nobody publishes official process plus concrete tasks together.

Loop: HackerRank 90 to 100 minutes with five questions (two LeetCode-easy, two HTML/CSS multiple-select, one React component; three reports from 2021 agree); recruiter call with two or three technical questions and A/B-testing or metrics questions; one or two live UI-coding rounds with two engineers, vanilla JS, HTML and CSS, sometimes React; system design for seniors; fit or business interview. Recurring tasks: autocomplete or typeahead search box (four independent reports: Taro Turkey 2021, Code360 2023-04, enginebogie 2023-05, Glassdoor; details: live dropdown, highlight matched text, debounce or throttle, caching, keyboard navigation, error states); a tooltip attachable to any DOM node; a form plus a generic validator; flatten an array or re-implement a lodash array function; fetch from an API and render a component (Taro 2023-07, Glassdoor 2022, Blind 2021); a dynamic form in React (2025); a dropdown with 300 ms hover intent; event delegation; a stock widget polling an API; business questions "what would you measure on a booking confirmation page" and "propose three improvements to booking.com and how to measure them". Candidates repeatedly complain about "pixel-perfect" grading and unclear expectations.

## Catalog mapping (2026-10-17)

Method: every catalog item (460: coding, trivia and debug across six technologies plus 23 system-design cases) was dumped with id, access, tags and companies, then matched by keyword against the recurring tasks in the source research, and the candidates were read back by title. Match grades: direct = the reported prompt is the item; practice version = same exercise in a different framework or a close relative (the `sources[].note` must say so); weak = only a shared topic, not proposed.

Mechanics that apply to this step (verified in code on 2026-10-17):

- A company page exists as soon as one catalog item carries its slug in `companies` (`generate-prerender-routes.mjs` collects slugs from coding, trivia, debug and system-design lists; `CompanyPublicDataService.loadIndex` lists any slug with `counts.all > 0`). Tag-only pages render the `catalogPreview` template: "<Label> Frontend Interview Questions Preview", counts, sample rows, and a "Public interview reports" block fed by `cdn/questions/company-reports.json`, which `gen-company-reports.mjs` builds from `sources[]` entries whose `company` matches the item's `companies`.
- `sources[]` keys are `label, url, publisher, date, note, company` (`question-sources-lib.mjs`); `company` must be one of the entry's `companies`. One to six sources per item, label 8 to 120 characters, note up to 280, `date` as `YYYY-MM`, https only, and the label or note must not contain "known questions" or "candidate reports".
- Unknown slugs render with a derived label (`booking-com` would read "Booking Com"), so `COMPANY_BRANDS` in `frontend/src/app/shared/company-branding.ts` needs an entry per new slug (label, optional `logoUrl` under `/assets/images/company-logos/`, otherwise a monogram), and `company-branding.spec.ts` ("has registry entries for all currently tagged companies") needs the four slugs added to its list.
- The hard-coded lists in `seo-content-inventory.mjs`, `seo-date-parity.mjs`, `audit-internal-link-equity.mjs` and `authored-companies.mjs` only cover authored pages; tag-only pages need none of them, and `seo-smoke.mjs` has no per-company block for them.
- Pipeline after tagging: `cd frontend && npm run gen:data && npm run gen:prerender && node scripts/update-question-id-manifest.mjs && node scripts/gen-company-reports.mjs && node scripts/lint-questions.mjs`. Adding `sources` to an entry that the backend interview registry pins by sha256 breaks `generate:interview-content:check` and needs a re-pin with a maintenance note (see the 1907f25b precedent). Adding a field to a free entry re-dates it in the sitemap. Local builds rewrite `cdn/data-version.json`.
- Slug for Booking.com: `booking-com` (what `normalizeCompanySlug('Booking.com')` produces, so any future source string normalises to it). Alternative `booking` is shorter but would need an alias.
- Pinned items among the proposals (checked on 2026-10-17 in `backend/content/interview/interview-coding-registry-v1.public.json`): `react-debounced-search`, `js-debounce`, `react-shopping-cart`, `js-abortable-helpers`, `js-once`, `js-unique-array`, `js-group-by`, `react-contact-form-starter`. Tagging them means one re-pin commit; the other proposed items are unpinned.
